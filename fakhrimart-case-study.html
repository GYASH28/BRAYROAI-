01 / THE BUSINESS REALITY

Not a fake ecommerce store.

The problem was not “make yarn look pretty.” It was making a large, changing craft catalogue understandable without pretending the website knew things the business still confirms manually.

FakhriMart serves customers looking for yarns, crochet and decorative threads, embroidery materials, macrame cord, beads, bases, purse materials and craft accessories. Some buyers want one item; others are resellers or bulk customers. Current shades, pack details, stock and quantity pricing can change, so a conventional add-to-cart flow would have risked showing false certainty.

BRAYROAI shaped the experience around a more truthful model: help the customer narrow the catalogue, understand the material, collect what they want, and then turn that intent into a useful WhatsApp enquiry the store can actually fulfil.

CORE PRODUCT PRINCIPLEDesign the digital journey around verified information — and make the human confirmation step feel intentional rather than like a limitation.

02 / CATALOGUE ARCHITECTURE

Turn variety into a path.

The source material contained many product families and older assumptions accumulated through development. The current system was cleaned around the supplier-confirmed range and reorganised so discovery starts with meaningful material groups instead of a wall of unrelated product cards.

GROUP

Material-first categories.

Yarns, threads, macrame and cords, embroidery, accessories, beads, bases and purse-making materials are treated as browseable families with their own context.

VERIFY

Remove unsupported catalogue assumptions.

Legacy variants, filters and structured-data claims were stripped back when the business could not verify them. The public catalogue stays inside the range the client can stand behind.

DISCOVER

Multiple ways into the same catalogue.

Customers can browse products directly, start from a project/use case, compare material families, return to recently viewed items, or use a guided finder when they do not know the product name.

03 / DECISION SUPPORT

Browsing should reduce questions.

Instead of trying to replace the store with a generic checkout template, the interface helps someone arrive at a better question. Product detail, colour preview, comparison, wishlist and guide flows prepare the enquiry before it reaches the team.

PRODUCT DETAIL

Give each material enough context.

Dedicated routes explain the product family, intended use and available supplier-confirmed information instead of forcing every decision into a tiny catalogue card.

COLOUR

Preview the direction, confirm the exact shade.

Interactive colour previews make exploration more tactile while the copy still tells customers to request the latest shade card or stock photo before finalising.

MEMORY

Let customers build intent over time.

Wishlist and recently viewed states help customers compare a changing catalogue without needing to remember every product they opened.

04 / CONVERSION

From browsing to a useful enquiry.

The conversion path is deliberately enquiry-led. The website does the organisation first; WhatsApp handles the volatile commerce details the business needs to confirm personally.

01 / FINDChoose a material

Start from category, search, project need or a guided recommendation.

02 / UNDERSTANDOpen the product

Review use, family, visual reference and the information that is actually verified.

03 / SPECIFYAdd intent

Select quantity context, shade direction and the items that belong in one enquiry.

04 / SENDBuild the WhatsApp request

Carry useful product context into the conversation instead of sending “price?” with no detail.

05 / CONFIRMVerify current details

The store confirms stock, current shades, pack information, delivery and quantity pricing.

05 / RESPONSIVE EXPERIENCE

One catalogue. Different hands.

The interface was built for both desktop comparison and phone-native enquiry behaviour. Mobile is not a compressed desktop view: navigation, product actions, discovery density and image behaviour are tuned around a smaller screen and touch input.

06 / ENGINEERING + QA

The browser had to keep up.

The project is a real React catalogue application, not a static visual mockup. Routes and heavier page modules are lazy loaded, SEO/supporting pages have dedicated flows, and regression scripts cover the things most likely to break as the catalogue grows.

07 / OUTCOME

A live catalogue system.

The result is a live digital storefront that makes a broad craft range easier to explore and turns customer intent into a clearer conversation with the business. It can grow with new verified products and content without forcing the client into invented real-time ecommerce claims.
